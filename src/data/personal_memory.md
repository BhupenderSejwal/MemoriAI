## 6. BOOK NOTES & ANALYSIS
### The Lord of the Rings (J.R.R. Tolkien) — Summary + Key Sections
*(Pretend-read version, optimized for semantic memory)*

#### Overall Summary
“The Lord of the Rings” tells the story of Frodo Baggins, who inherits the One Ring and must destroy it in Mount Doom to save Middle-earth. The story is divided into three books:
1. The Fellowship of the Ring  
2. The Two Towers  
3. The Return of the King  

Themes:
- The corrupting nature of power  
- Friendship, loyalty, sacrifice  
- Hope in darkness  

#### Important Sections
- **The Shire** — peaceful beginning; symbolizes innocence.  
- **Council of Elrond** — Fellowship is formed.  
- **Moria / Balrog Scene** — Gandalf sacrifices himself ("You shall not pass!").  
- **Gollum’s dual personality** — key psychological element.  
- **Shelob’s Lair** — Frodo captured, Sam shows ultimate loyalty.  
- **Battle of Pelennor Fields** — turning point in the war.  
- **Mount Doom** — Frodo fails at the end; Gollum unintentionally saves Middle-earth.  

#### Author Notes
- J.R.R. Tolkien was a linguist and invented full languages (Elvish).  
- The story is influenced by Norse mythology, WW1, and his academic work in Oxford.

#### Favorite Highlights (pretend)
- Friendship between Frodo and Sam.  
- Tragic story of Boromir.  
- The symbolism of light vs shadow.
